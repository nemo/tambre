03 — Ledger

Slug: 03-ledger Date: 2026-08-08 Parent: none

One line

The registry as a ruled account book: one line per pack, seventeen sortable columns, a real 16×3 support matrix drawn at 4 px per cell, and expandable sub-rows where every bound hook is its own line with its own gain, its own waveform, and its own sound under the cursor.

The seven axes

AxisThis variant
Browse metaphorFinancial ledger / spreadsheet. You are reconciling 26 packs against 16 events, not shopping. Row ordinals continue across pages like an account book's line numbers.
TypographyMonospace everywhere — titles, scopes, tags, numbers. font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums globally. 11 px, 1.32 line-height. Approach and trust are single letterforms (T J B H / · † ‡), never coloured pills.
Density19 px rows, 2 px vertical padding. 20 rows in the default feed, 25 per page with the trust filter open, rows: all for 26 + 220 hook lines = 246 lines on one scroll. The densest variant in the library.
ColourTwo functional hues on a near-white ground (#fdfdfc). Indigo #2323a8 marks the sorted column — header label, caret, bottom hairline, and the cells in that column. Ledger red #b3261e marks the row that is currently sounding. Nothing else is coloured.
OrnamentHairlines only (#e4e4e0 cell rules, #c6c6c0 section rules). Zebra striping on alternate pack rows. No cards, no shadows, no radii, no gradients, no fills behind text. The only graphics are the coverage matrix and the waveform strips, and both are data.
MotionNone. No transitions, no animations, no keyframes anywhere in the file. The playing row gets a 90 ms class swap with no easing — a single blink, like a terminal cell repainting.
Audio interactionHover any line to audition it; pack lines play their primary cue, hook lines play exactly that hook's sound at that hook's gain. space plays the cursor line, [ / ] sweep down a pack's hooks auditioning each. Hover has its own 100 ms throttle, separate from the role throttle.

Load-bearing decisions

The hook is a row, not a detail view. The most important requirement was making each bound event individually auditionable. In a table the honest answer is a sub-row per hook. Expanding a pack inserts one <tr class="hook"> per binding — 17 cells, same column grid as the parent, so the eye can still travel straight down. Each hook line carries the canonical event name verbatim (permission.request, not "when it asks"), the sound name, the role letter, the gain to two decimals, the throttle in ms, the rendered length in ms, its fallback chain, and its own 88×13 waveform. Hovering it plays it. ] walks down the list. Sweeping a nine-hook pack takes about two seconds.

Columns are reused, not added, on hook rows. SND → gain, HK → throttle, PLAY → length, TAGS → fallback chain. The legend says so. This keeps the grid to 17 columns instead of 25 and preserves vertical alignment: gains stack under sound counts, both right-aligned tabular, so a pack's gain staging reads as a column of numbers.

The coverage matrix is drawn at true resolution. 16 events × 3 agents × 4 px = 64×12 px of inline SVG per row. Filled square = direct native support. Hollow square = reachable only through the declared fallback chain. Blank = the pack doesn't bind that event at all. This is the one place the variant spends pixels, because it is the one thing a comparison table can do that a card grid cannot: 26 packs × 48 cells visible simultaneously, 488 native cells and 11 fallback-only cells in the current fixture. The C X U numeric columns count direct native only — no fallback padding, which is why they read 8/8/9 and 14/11/9 rather than 16/16/16.

A hook row's coverage strip is drawn at its own event's x-offset. Not centred, not left-aligned — offset by EIX[event] * 4 inside the same 64 px viewBox. Expand a pack and the sub-rows' 3-cell strips fall exactly under their own columns in the parent matrix. Free alignment discipline; it makes the matrix legible instead of decorative.

The gesture gate is a sign-off cell. Top right of the masthead: audio ☐ not armed — sign here, dotted underline. Clicking it ticks the box and the label becomes audio ☑ armed · 44100 Hz mono · master −1.9 dBFS. Any keypress or click in the table also arms it. Framed as a certification line in a ledger head rather than an apology banner. Waveforms and the whole matrix render before arming, so the sheet is fully readable with the audio off.

Hover throttle is 100 ms and separate from role throttle. turn.end is role cue → role throttle 0, which is correct for a live agent and ruinous for a 25-row table. A 20-row mouse sweep at 5 ms/row fires exactly one sound; the same sweep at 150 ms/row fires all of them. Role throttle still applies on top (churn 120 ms), so hammering a tool.pre line is suppressed independently.

Unverified is genuinely withheld. Six of 26 packs are unverified and the default feed shows 20. The trust filter is a five-way toggle (default / curated / attested / unverified / all); u toggles default↔all. The masthead states the arithmetic out loud: "26 packs · 91 sounds · 20 in default feed (6 unverified withheld)". Every other filter composes with it — filtering to bed still hides unverified beds.

Every column earns its sort. Fifteen sortable keys including two that only a table would offer: COVERAGE sorts by total direct bindings across all three agents, and WAVE sorts by the rendered length in ms of the pack's primary sound. Strings default ascending, numbers and dates descending, second click reverses, ties break on @scope/name so ordering is stable.

What works

  • Comparison actually happens. Sort by U, then by C, and @scrivener/cursor-typist (C=8 X=8 U=9) visibly moves. That fact is invisible in a card grid and obvious here.
  • The matrix reads as a texture. Packs that bind only the boundary events show a dense block on the left and nothing past column 8. Packs that reach into notification / permission.denied show a Claude-only stripe. You learn the shape of the event taxonomy by scanning the column, not by reading the docs.
  • Hook sweeping is fast and unambiguous. Expand, put the mouse at the top of the sub-rows, drag down. Each canonical event name sits next to the sound you are hearing at the gain you will actually get. This is the clearest hook-to-sound mapping of the three directions so far.
  • Zero-ornament light UI turns out to be the right ground for 246 lines. No shadow to accumulate, no radius to make the grid ragged, no fill to fight the two functional hues.
  • Fallback-only reachability being visually distinct stops the coverage number from being a lie. Eleven hollow cells across the fixture; every one is a permission.request → notification → turn.end chain landing on Codex or Cursor.

What doesn't

  • 17 columns will not survive a narrow window. Below roughly 1200 px the table horizontally scrolls, and the horizontal scroll is inside #scroll, which also owns the vertical scroll. The sticky header stays put vertically but the # and PACK columns are not sticky horizontally, so scrolling right orphans the numbers from their names. A real build needs position: sticky; left: 0 on the first three columns. I did not do it and the omission is load-bearing on a laptop.
  • This is unusable on a phone. Not "compromised" — unusable. No responsive path exists; a 375 px viewport shows about four columns. The other two variants degrade; this one just doesn't fit. If the library needs one mobile-viable dense view, it isn't this one.
  • Truncation loses the long title. "Quiet Office at Four in the Morning, With Radiators" clips at 330 px with an ellipsis and a title tooltip. A tooltip is not a design solution; the ledger form structurally punishes any pack whose name is prose. Card variants get to wrap. A publisher reading this would rename their pack, which means the layout is editing the registry's content, which is bad.
  • The trust glyphs are too quiet. · / † / ‡ at 11 px in a 20 px-wide column are nearly indistinguishable at a glance, and the difference between "unverified" and "curated" is the single most consequential fact on the row. Committing to letterforms over colour was the right call for approach (T J B H are unmistakable) and the wrong call for trust. The honest fix is probably a fourth column of literal words, at a real density cost.
  • Sorted-column tinting fights the playing row. Both are colour on text. When a red playing row intersects the indigo sorted column I override to red, so for 90 ms the sorted column loses its cue. Two text hues on one axis was one hue too many; the sorted column should have been a background wash, which the no-fills rule forbade.
  • Zebra striping breaks around expansions. Stripes are assigned per pack row in sequence, so an expanded pack's hook block sits between two same-coloured stripes and the alternation visually stutters. Correct behaviour is to stripe by pack group, tinting the hook rows with the parent. Not done.
  • **space scroll-jacking.** space plays the cursor row and calls preventDefault, which removes page-down-by-space. In a table this dense that is a real loss, and n/p paging is not the same gesture.
  • No pack page, no publisher page, no install command. The spec's /@scope/name route is exactly what the expanded sub-rows replace — which is arguably the strongest idea here, but it means there is nowhere to put a README, a version history, or the copy-ready tambre install line. A ledger has no room for prose.
  • Sorting re-renders the whole tbody, which re-draws up to 246 canvases. Peaks are cached so it is a few ms, but it also collapses nothing and preserves nothing about scroll position. On a real 10k-row registry this architecture is wrong; it wants virtual scrolling.
  • **native-complete on: claude / codex / cursor** is a defensible filter but an opaque label. It means "every event this pack binds is directly supported", which is strict enough that Cursor matches only 2 of 26 packs. Users will read it as "works on Cursor" and conclude wrongly.

Burned for future variants

  • The 16×3 micro-matrix is reusable and cheap — 64×12 px of SVG, three visual states, no interaction required. Any variant that needs honest coverage should steal it; it fits in a card footer as easily as a table cell.
  • Sub-row-per-hook is the strongest hook-auditioning affordance I have found. Anything with a list spine should consider it. The key detail is that the sub-row keeps the parent's column grid rather than becoming a free-form panel.
  • Hover throttle ≠ role throttle is not variant-specific; it is a bug waiting in every design. 100 ms is a good number. Bake it into the shared transport.
  • Trust-by-letterform does not work. Don't repeat it. Approach-by-letterform does.
  • The sign-off gesture gate (a ledger certification line you tick) generalises to any metaphor with a document surface — a stamp, an initial, a signature rule. Better than a banner.
  • Do not attempt a denser variant. 19 px rows at 11 px type with 17 columns is the floor before the sheet stops being scannable. Density is now a solved axis for this library; spend the next variant elsewhere.
  • Two text hues on one grid is one too many. Reserve colour-on-text for exactly one job.
